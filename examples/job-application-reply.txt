Dear Gulfmarsh Land Trust hiring team,

I'm applying for the Director of Land Stewardship role at Gulfmarsh Land Trust. I have 13 years in wetland habitat and waterfowl work, and at the South Texas Refuge Complex I plan water levels, plantings and prescribed burns with refuge staff.

In August 2024 I was on a waterfowl banding detail in Saskatchewan, with a crew of 4. On our drives between trap sites, there was time for reflection. I thought about how vastly different this work was from my day-to-day job. It came full circle for me to see thousands of ducks on their breeding grounds. They would not be so numerous and healthy without the habitat we provided at the South Texas Refuge Complex, about 2,000 miles away, and other refuges like ours. I came out of this experience with numerous cuts, bruises, achy joints and muscles, but also mentally refreshed and stronger than ever. I had a renewed sense of purpose. I knew I personally contributed to an integral link in the chain of conservation.

Please let me know if you'd like to talk.

Renata Szabo-Quill

What I used

- The first paragraph comes from your resume and the posting. The 13 years is the number in your resume header. The water levels, plantings and prescribed burns line is your South Texas Refuge Complex bullet, worded the same way.
- The second paragraph is your banding detail story, in your words. The date, Saskatchewan, the crew of 4 and the 2,000 miles come from your resume line. The drives between trap sites, the ducks, the cuts and bruises and the renewed sense of purpose come from what you wrote. I split a few long sentences and changed "knowing that I personally contributed" to "I knew I personally contributed". I added no facts.
- I left out your sentences about how easy it is to get caught up in local work and forget why it matters. They say something that is always true, and the letter can only hold so many of those before it starts to read like a form letter.
- I left out the rest of your resume bullets because your resume goes with the letter.
- The letter is short on purpose, 185 words. It rests on one story, and I didn't pad it. One more story from you makes room for a longer letter.
- I ran the checker script that came with my instructions. It found no rule failures. It can't judge facts, so I checked those by reading against your resume and notes.

What I still need from you

1. Why do you want this job? Nothing in the letter says, and I didn't make a reason up.
2. The posting asks for supervising experience and leads a staff of 5 plus a seasonal crew. Your resume shows none. Did you supervise anyone, at the refuge or on the banding detail? Does "crew of 4" include you, and did you lead it?
3. The posting asks about water control structures. Your resume says you plan water levels. Did you run structures such as gates or pumps, and which ones? I didn't say you did.
4. The posting asks for grant work and comfort with funders and board members. Your resume shows neither. Did you write or carry a grant, or present to a funder or a board?
5. Your resume says you meet with neighboring landowners and the state wildlife agency. The posting wants landowner and duck hunting club partnerships. Tell me about one meeting that went somewhere: who was there, what you did first, what came of it.
6. Does the 13 years in your header include the 2012 to 2017 technician job? I wrote "13 years in wetland habitat and waterfowl work" and didn't call it years of managing.

Any one of 3, 4 or 5, told like you told the banding detail, would let me add a second story and a line tying it to the posting.
